Scope, title and workshop-fit note

The current title, “Beyond Recall: Failure Semantics of Long-Term Memory in LLM Agents,” can suggest broad agent or semantic-memory evidence. “Recovery Semantics of Memory Mutations: A Synthetic Factorial Study” better states the measured contribution: a local, controlled mechanism experiment. Keep the absence of live-model memory-quality, distributed-storage failure and general concurrency evidence explicit.

A plausible target is a workshop or artifact/case-study track whose actual call welcomes reproducible systems or AI-engineering mechanism studies. This is a conditional fit assessment, not an acceptance or impact prediction. The contribution is the transparent factor decomposition and counterexamples; identity, logged replay, receipts and much fault-injection methodology are established prior art. A venue requiring a new algorithm or broad production/semantic evaluation may expect substantially different evidence.

No venue has been selected. Once one is named, inspect its official call and rules for scope, page limits, anonymity, AI-assistance disclosure, and artifact access.
